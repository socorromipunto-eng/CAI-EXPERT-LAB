# CAI USA — Information Technology Labs (Non-Normative)

## Overview

This directory contains **non-normative laboratory artifacts** for the **CAI USA Information
Technology (IT) sector**. These laboratories demonstrate how CAI concepts are applied within
enterprise, cloud, and infrastructure IT environments in a **controlled, operationally
safe, and human-governed** manner.

The Information Technology labs exist to validate **design intent**, **governance boundaries**,
**evidentiary handling**, and **mandatory human authority** without modifying CAI CORE,
the CAI USA baseline, or asserting regulatory or operational compliance.

---

## Non-Normative Scope

All artifacts contained in this directory are **laboratory demonstrations only**.

They:
- Do **not** define operational procedures or remediation playbooks
- Do **not** authorize configuration changes or enforcement actions
- Do **not** assert compliance with NIST, ISO, SOC, or other IT standards
- Do **not** replace enterprise governance, change management, or operational controls

Normative CAI documentation remains authoritative at all times.

---

## Purpose of Information Technology Labs

The Information Technology laboratories are designed to demonstrate:

- CCES-compliant telemetry normalization across heterogeneous IT systems
- State-aware correlation preserving service availability and operational context
- Governed telemetry flow and evidence generation
- **Strictly advisory, human-governed decision support**
- Explicit **AI governance and non-autonomy**

These labs emphasize that **service availability, operational continuity, and human authority
dominate all analysis and decision-support activities**.

---

## Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence is present in all Information Technology labs as a **governed
analytical support component**.

Across all Information Technology laboratories:
- AI is **strictly advisory**
- AI does **not** execute remediation or enforcement actions
- AI does **not** advance lifecycle states
- AI does **not** override operational authority
- AI behavior is **bounded, explainable, and auditable**

AI exists to support human operators in complex IT environments, not to automate operations.

## Information Technology Laboratory Artifacts

The Information Technology sector includes the following laboratory artifacts:

- **CCES Normalization**  
  `CAI-USA-Information-Technology-Lab-CCES-Normalization.md`  
  Demonstrates deterministic normalization of IT telemetry into CCES while preserving
  service context, trust boundaries, and evidentiary traceability.

- **State-Aware Correlation**  
  `CAI-USA-Information-Technology-Lab-State-Aware-Correlation.md`  
  Demonstrates correlation of CCES-normalized telemetry with explicit awareness of
  application state, service dependencies, and operational continuity.

- **Telemetry Flow and Evidence**  
  `CAI-USA-Information-Technology-Lab-Telemetry-Flow-and-Evidence.md`  
  Demonstrates governed end-to-end telemetry flow and evidence generation, ensuring chain-of-
  custody, integrity, and audit readiness in IT environments.

- **Human-Governed Decision Support**  
  `CAI-USA-Information-Technology-Lab-Human-Governed-Decision-Support.md`  
  Demonstrates strictly advisory, explainable decision support with mandatory human authority
  and explicit non-autonomy.
