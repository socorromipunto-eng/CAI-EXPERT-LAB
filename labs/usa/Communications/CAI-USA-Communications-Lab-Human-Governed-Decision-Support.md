## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate how CAI provides structured, explainable, and state-aware decision support for Communications environments while preserving mandatory human authority.  
This lab explicitly proves that CAI does not perform autonomous enforcement and that all high-impact decisions remain under human governance.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Communications non-production environments only.  
It does not execute isolation, eviction, restoration, or service-affecting actions.  
The lab focuses exclusively on decision-support preparation.

## Decision Support Philosophy in CAI

## AI Role and Governance in Decision Support

In this laboratory, Artificial Intelligence is used strictly as a governed analytical support component under CAI Core integrity and governance rules.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Pattern analysis across CCES-normalized telemetry  
• Confidence scoring and uncertainty estimation  
• Contextual enrichment based on historical and state-aware data  
• Explainable correlation support  

AI does not:

• Trigger lifecycle state transitions  
• Execute containment, isolation, or remediation actions  
• Override human authority  
• Operate outside CAI Engine governance  

All AI outputs are explicitly bounded, explainable, and treated as advisory inputs only.

AI behavior is subject to:

• CAI-Core-Integrity-and-Self-Defense-Governance  
• CAI-Core-Operational-State-Model  
• CAI USA AI governance constraints  
• Mandatory human-in-the-loop validation  

This laboratory demonstrates AI as a constrained, auditable, and non-autonomous decision-support mechanism aligned with CAI principles.

CAI is designed to support decisions, not replace them.

In Communications contexts:

• Service continuity dominates speed  
• Explainability dominates automation  
• Human authority is non-negotiable  

Decision support exists to reduce cognitive load, not to delegate responsibility.

## Inputs to Decision Support

Decision support consumes:

• CCES-normalized telemetry  
• State-aware correlation outputs  
• Asset and service criticality context  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  
• Operational and availability constraints  

No single signal is sufficient to drive decisions.

## Decision Support Outputs

CAI produces structured decision-support artifacts including:

• Summary of observed conditions  
• Lifecycle state context  
• Risk and impact framing  
• Possible response options  
• Explicit non-recommended actions  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  

Outputs are descriptive, not prescriptive.

## Human Authority Enforcement

All decision-support outputs require explicit human review.

CAI does not:

• Trigger enforcement  
• Advance lifecycle states  
• Approve actions  
• Override operational authority  

Human decision-makers retain full accountability.

## Evidence and Auditability

Decision-support artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Inputs considered  
• Correlation rationale  
• Model or rule references  
• Time and state context  
• Human acknowledgment requirement  

Evidence supports audit, investigation, and regulatory scrutiny.

## Communications-Specific Constraints

In Communications environments:

• Decision support must favor service stability  
• Ambiguity defaults to observation  
• Availability and continuity override remediation urgency  
• Human review is mandatory for escalation  

Decision support must never pressure operators toward action.
