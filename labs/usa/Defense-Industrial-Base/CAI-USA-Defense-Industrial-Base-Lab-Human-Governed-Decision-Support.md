## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate how CAI provides structured, explainable,
and state-aware decision support for Defense Industrial Base environments while preserving
mandatory human authority, national security sensitivity, supply chain trust, and explicit
AI governance.

This lab explicitly demonstrates that CAI does not perform autonomous enforcement and that
all mission-, program-, and contract-impacting decisions remain under human governance.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Defense Industrial Base non-production environments,
including defense contractors, subcontractors, suppliers, research entities, and supporting
IT, OT, and engineering systems.

This lab does not:

• Execute mission, production, or operational actions  
• Interact with classified systems or mission execution platforms  
• Trigger containment, isolation, or remediation  
• Advance operational or lifecycle states  
• Infer or expose classified or export-controlled information  

The lab focuses exclusively on decision-support preparation and analysis.

## Decision Support Philosophy in CAI

CAI decision support exists to assist cleared human operators by structuring complex,
sensitivity-aware information, not to automate or replace human judgment.

In Defense Industrial Base contexts:

• National security sensitivity dominates speed  
• Supply chain and program integrity dominate urgency  
• Explainability dominates automation  
• Human authority is non-negotiable  

Decision support must never pressure operators toward action or inference.

## AI Role and Governance in Decision Support

In this laboratory, Artificial Intelligence is used strictly as a governed analytical support
component under CAI Core integrity and governance rules.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Synthesizing CCES-normalized telemetry and correlation artifacts  
• Supporting risk framing and consequence analysis  
• Providing confidence and uncertainty indicators  
• Highlighting state-dependent trust, clearance, and program constraints  

AI does not:

• Trigger lifecycle state transitions  
• Execute blocking, isolation, or remediation actions  
• Override human authority  
• Operate outside CAI Engine governance  
• Infer classified intent or mission outcomes  

All AI outputs are explicitly bounded, explainable, auditable, and treated as advisory inputs only.

AI behavior is subject to:

• CAI-Core-Integrity-and-Self-Defense-Governance  
• CAI-Core-Operational-State-Model  
• CAI USA AI governance constraints  
• Mandatory human-in-the-loop validation  

## Inputs to Decision Support

Decision support consumes:

• CCES-normalized telemetry  
• State-aware correlation artifacts  
• Program, contract, and asset criticality context  
• Trust-domain and clearance constraints  
• Supply chain and dependency indicators  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  

No single signal is sufficient to drive decisions.

## Decision Support Outputs

CAI produces structured decision-support artifacts including:

• Summary of observed conditions  
• Operational and lifecycle state context  
• Program and supply chain risk framing  
• Possible response options for human consideration  
• Explicit non-recommended actions  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  
• Sensitivity and handling annotations  

Outputs are descriptive, not prescriptive.

## Human Authority Enforcement

All decision-support outputs require explicit human review by authorized personnel.

CAI does not:

• Trigger enforcement  
• Advance lifecycle states  
• Approve actions  
• Override operational or program authority  

Human decision-makers retain full accountability.

## Evidence and Auditability

Decision-support artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Inputs considered  
• Correlation rationale  
• AI-assisted analytical references  
• Time, state, and sensitivity context  
• Human acknowledgment requirement  

Evidence supports audit, investigation, and forensic reconstruction under Defense Industrial
Base governance expectations.

## Defense Industrial Base–Specific Constraints

In DIB environments:

• National security sensitivity overrides remediation urgency  
• Supply chain trust and program integrity dominate decision framing  
• Ambiguity defaults to observation  
• Human review is mandatory for escalation  

Decision support must never introduce operational or analytical pressure.
