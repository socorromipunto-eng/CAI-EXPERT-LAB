## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate the normalization of heterogeneous
Defense Industrial Base (DIB) telemetry into the CAI Common Cyber Evidence Schema (CCES)
while preserving national security sensitivity, program integrity, supply chain trust,
evidentiary traceability, and explicit AI governance.

This lab validates that DIB telemetry can be normalized deterministically and preserved as
evidentiary-grade artifacts without enabling autonomous execution, enforcement, or
decision authority.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Defense Industrial Base non-production environments,
including defense contractors, subcontractors, suppliers, research entities, and
supporting IT, OT, and engineering systems.

This lab does not:

• Execute production, engineering, or operational actions  
• Interact with classified systems or mission execution platforms  
• Trigger containment, isolation, or remediation  
• Modify configurations, access controls, or program states  

The lab focuses exclusively on telemetry normalization and evidence integrity.

## CCES Normalization in CAI

CCES normalization establishes a consistent, governed representation of telemetry
originating from heterogeneous DIB sources across IT, OT, engineering, and supply chain
systems.

Normalized telemetry preserves:

• Source provenance and system identity  
• Temporal accuracy and sequencing  
• Program, contract, and asset criticality context  
• Supply chain and trust-domain indicators  
• Chain-of-custody for evidentiary use  

Normalization does not imply correlation, interpretation, or decision-making.

## AI Role and Governance in CCES Normalization

In this laboratory, Artificial Intelligence is present strictly as a governed support
component operating under CAI Core integrity and governance constraints.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Assisting telemetry classification and schema alignment  
• Supporting enrichment of contextual metadata  
• Identifying normalization inconsistencies or gaps  
• Providing confidence indicators on normalization completeness  

AI does not:

• Interpret mission, program, or operational outcomes  
• Correlate events across lifecycle states  
• Recommend actions, blocking, or enforcement  
• Trigger state transitions  
• Override deterministic normalization rules  
• Operate outside CAI governance boundaries  

All AI involvement is bounded, explainable, auditable, and strictly non-autonomous.

## Telemetry Inputs

The lab consumes representative DIB telemetry including:

• Engineering and development system logs  
• Access, identity, and authentication events  
• Network and boundary telemetry  
• Configuration, integrity, and change records  
• Supply chain and vendor interaction signals  

All inputs are treated as observational and sensitivity-aware.

## Normalized Outputs

The lab produces CCES-normalized telemetry artifacts including:

• Canonical event representations  
• Preserved source and timing context  
• Program, asset, and trust-domain identifiers  
• Sensitivity and handling annotations  
• Confidence indicators for normalization quality  

Outputs are descriptive and evidentiary, not prescriptive.

## Evidence and Auditability

Normalization artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Input source identification  
• Transformation and normalization steps  
• Applied schemas and rules  
• AI-assisted enrichment indicators  
• Time and state context  

These artifacts support audit, investigation, and forensic reconstruction under
Defense Industrial Base governance expectations.

## Defense Industrial Base–Specific Constraints

In DIB environments:

• National security sensitivity overrides analytic optimization  
• Supply chain integrity and trust dominate telemetry handling  
• Evidence integrity is prioritized over processing speed  
• Human governance is mandatory for schema evolution  

Normalization must never expose, infer, or act upon classified or mission-critical intent.
