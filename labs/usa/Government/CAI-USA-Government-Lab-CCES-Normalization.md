## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate the deterministic normalization of
heterogeneous Government-sector telemetry into the CAI Common Cyber Evidence Schema (CCES)
while preserving institutional integrity, public accountability, evidentiary traceability,
and explicit AI governance.

This laboratory validates that Government telemetry can be normalized in a manner that
supports transparency, auditability, and lawful oversight without enabling autonomous
execution, enforcement, or policy inference.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Government non-production environments, including civilian
federal agencies, state and local government systems, and inter-agency supporting IT and
communications infrastructures.

This lab explicitly excludes:

• Policy enforcement or interpretation  
• Automated decision-making affecting citizens or institutions  
• Execution of administrative, legal, or regulatory actions  
• Modification of system configurations or access controls  

The lab focuses exclusively on telemetry normalization and evidence integrity.

## Preconditions and Assumptions

• CAI Core and CAI USA baseline documents are authoritative and frozen.  
• Government telemetry sources are simulated or sanitized.  
• No classified, export-controlled, or privacy-sensitive data is processed.  
• Human authority governs all interpretation and downstream use of evidence.  
• This lab does not assert compliance with FISMA, FedRAMP, NIST, or other mandates.

## CCES Normalization in Government Context

CCES normalization provides a consistent, governed representation of telemetry originating
from heterogeneous Government systems, enabling traceable analysis while preserving public
trust and institutional boundaries.

Normalized telemetry preserves:

• Source provenance and agency context  
• Temporal accuracy and sequencing  
• System, service, and institutional criticality  
• Jurisdictional and oversight boundaries  
• Chain-of-custody for evidentiary use  

Normalization does not imply correlation, prioritization, risk scoring, or decision-making.

## AI Role and Governance in CCES Normalization

Artificial Intelligence is present strictly as a governed analytical support component,
operating under CAI Core integrity and governance constraints.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Assisting telemetry classification and schema alignment  
• Supporting enrichment of non-sensitive contextual metadata  
• Identifying normalization inconsistencies or gaps  
• Providing confidence indicators related to normalization completeness  

AI does not:

• Interpret policy, legal, or administrative intent  
• Correlate events across lifecycle states  
• Recommend enforcement, blocking, or corrective actions  
• Trigger state transitions  
• Override deterministic normalization rules  
• Operate outside CAI governance boundaries  

All AI involvement is bounded, explainable, auditable, and strictly non-autonomous.

## Telemetry Inputs

The laboratory consumes representative Government telemetry including:

• Identity and access management logs  
• Application and service audit logs  
• Network and boundary telemetry  
• Configuration and change records  
• Availability and integrity signals  

All inputs are treated as observational data only.

## Normalized Outputs

The lab produces CCES-normalized telemetry artifacts including:

• Canonical event representations  
• Preserved source, agency, and system context  
• Temporal and integrity metadata  
• Institutional sensitivity annotations  
• Confidence indicators for normalization quality  

Outputs are descriptive and evidentiary, not prescriptive.

## Evidence and Auditability

Normalization artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Input source identification and provenance  
• Transformation and normalization steps  
• Applied schemas and rules  
• AI-assisted enrichment indicators  
• Time and governance context  

These artifacts support audit, investigation, oversight, and lawful review.

## Government-Specific Constraints

In Government environments:

• Public accountability overrides analytic optimization  
• Evidence integrity and traceability dominate telemetry handling  
• Transparency and oversight boundaries must be preserved  
• Human governance is mandatory for schema evolution  

Normalization must never infer intent, policy direction, or enforcement posture.
