## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate the deterministic normalization of
heterogeneous Water and Wastewater sector telemetry into the CAI Common Cyber Evidence
Schema (CCES) while preserving public health protection, operational continuity, evidentiary
integrity, and explicit Artificial Intelligence (AI) governance.

This laboratory validates that telemetry originating from Water and Wastewater environments
can be normalized into CCES without loss of safety context, process meaning, provenance, or
auditability, and without enabling autonomous execution, public health inference, or
operational decision authority.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Water and Wastewater non-production environments, including:

• Drinking water treatment support systems  
• Wastewater treatment and collection support systems  
• SCADA-adjacent monitoring and historian platforms  
• Laboratory information management systems (LIMS)  
• Asset management and maintenance support systems  
• Supporting IT, OT, and safety-adjacent infrastructures  

This laboratory explicitly excludes:

• Interaction with live treatment, distribution, or control systems  
• Automated actions affecting water quality, treatment processes, or service delivery  
• Modification of operational, chemical, or configuration parameters  
• Advancement of operational or lifecycle states  
• Interpretation of water quality, safety margins, or regulatory thresholds  

The lab focuses exclusively on telemetry normalization and evidence integrity.

## Preconditions and Assumptions

• CAI Core and CAI USA baseline documents are authoritative and frozen.  
• The Water and Wastewater sector overlay and traceability matrix are authoritative.  
• All telemetry sources are simulated or sanitized.  
• No live public water systems are impacted.  
• No chemical dosing, flow control, or treatment logic is accessed.  
• Human authority governs all interpretation and downstream use of lab outputs.  
• This lab does not assert compliance with EPA, DHS, CISA, NIST, or state water authorities.

## CCES Normalization in Water and Wastewater Context

CCES normalization establishes a consistent, governed representation of telemetry originating
from heterogeneous Water and Wastewater systems while preserving public health sensitivity,
operational conservatism, and institutional accountability.

Normalized telemetry preserves:

• Source provenance and system identity  
• Temporal accuracy and sequencing  
• Public health sensitivity and service criticality  
• Process-adjacent operational context  
• Asset, facility, and service identifiers  
• Chain-of-custody for evidentiary use  

Normalization does not imply correlation, interpretation, prioritization, or operational
judgment. It prepares evidence only.

## AI Role and Governance in CCES Normalization

Artificial Intelligence is present strictly as a governed analytical support component
operating under CAI Core integrity and governance constraints.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Assisting telemetry classification and CCES schema alignment  
• Supporting enrichment of non-safety contextual metadata  
• Identifying normalization inconsistencies or schema gaps  
• Providing confidence indicators related to normalization completeness  

AI does not:

• Interpret water quality, treatment efficacy, or safety margins  
• Correlate events across lifecycle states  
• Recommend operational, chemical, or safety actions  
• Trigger state transitions or alerts  
• Override deterministic normalization rules  
• Operate outside CAI governance boundaries  

All AI involvement is bounded, explainable, auditable, and strictly non-autonomous.

## Telemetry Inputs

This laboratory consumes representative Water and Wastewater telemetry including:

• Identity and access management logs  
• Operator access and authentication events  
• SCADA-adjacent monitoring telemetry (read-only)  
• Sensor and analyzer status signals (non-control)  
• Laboratory and sampling system logs  
• Network, infrastructure, and boundary telemetry  
• Configuration, integrity, and availability indicators  

All telemetry is treated as observational and public-health–sensitive data.

## CCES Normalization Process

Telemetry is normalized according to CAI-USA-CCES principles:

• Each event is assigned a unique CCES event identifier  
• Source system, asset identity, and trust domain are declared  
• Time synchronization and clock provenance are recorded  
• Public health sensitivity annotations are preserved  
• Transformation steps are logged and reproducible  
• No destructive aggregation or semantic compression is permitted  

Normalization explicitly avoids abstracting process telemetry into generic “security events”
that would obscure public health or operational meaning.

## Normalized Outputs

The laboratory produces CCES-normalized telemetry artifacts including:

• Canonical CCES event records  
• Preserved source, facility, and timing context  
• Public health sensitivity annotations  
• Asset, system, and service identifiers  
• Confidence indicators for normalization quality  

Outputs are descriptive and evidentiary, not prescriptive.

## Evidence and Auditability

Normalization artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Input source identification and provenance  
• Transformation and normalization steps  
• Applied CCES schemas and rules  
• AI-assisted enrichment indicators  
• Time, state, and governance context  

These artifacts support audit, investigation, and forensic reconstruction.

## Water and Wastewater–Specific Constraints

In Water and Wastewater environments:

• Public health protection overrides analytic optimization  
• Conservative handling dominates telemetry processing  
• Evidence integrity is prioritized over processing speed  
• Human governance is mandatory for schema evolution  

Normalization must never infer water safety status, compliance posture, or operational
readiness.
