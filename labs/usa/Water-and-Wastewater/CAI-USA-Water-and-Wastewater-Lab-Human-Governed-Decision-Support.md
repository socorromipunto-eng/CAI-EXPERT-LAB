## Purpose

The purpose of this laboratory is to demonstrate how CAI provides structured, explainable,
and state-aware decision support for Water and Wastewater environments while preserving
public health primacy, operational conservatism, institutional accountability, and
mandatory human authority.

This laboratory explicitly demonstrates that CAI does not perform autonomous treatment
control, operational enforcement, public health determination, or safety decision-making,
and that all consequential actions remain under explicit human governance.

## Scope

This laboratory applies to USA Water and Wastewater non-production environments, including:

• Drinking water treatment support systems  
• Wastewater treatment and collection support systems  
• SCADA-adjacent monitoring and historian platforms  
• Laboratory information management systems (LIMS)  
• Asset management and maintenance support systems  
• Supporting IT, OT, and safety-adjacent infrastructures  

This laboratory explicitly excludes:

• Treatment, distribution, or process control decisions  
• Automated actions affecting water quality or service delivery  
• Modification of operational, chemical, or configuration parameters  
• Advancement of operational or lifecycle states  
• Interpretation of water quality, safety margins, or regulatory thresholds  

The lab focuses exclusively on decision-support preparation and analysis.

## Preconditions and Assumptions

• CAI Core and CAI USA baseline documents are authoritative and frozen.  
• Decision support consumes evidentiary-grade, CCES-normalized telemetry only.  
• Telemetry sources are simulated or sanitized.  
• No live public water systems are impacted.  
• No chemical dosing, flow control, or treatment logic is accessed.  
• Human authority is mandatory for all interpretations and decisions.  
• This lab does not assert compliance with EPA, DHS, CISA, NIST, or state water authorities.

## Decision Support Philosophy in Water and Wastewater Context

CAI decision support exists to assist human operators, engineers, and analysts by structuring
complex, safety- and public-health–sensitive information—not to automate or replace water
system management judgment.

In Water and Wastewater environments:

• Public health protection dominates speed  
• Explainability dominates automation  
• Human authority is non-negotiable  
• Ambiguity defaults to observation and documentation  

Decision support must never pressure operators toward action or inference.

## AI Role and Governance in Decision Support

In this laboratory, Artificial Intelligence is used strictly as a governed analytical support
component under CAI Core integrity and governance rules.

AI capabilities are limited to:

• Synthesizing CCES-normalized telemetry and correlation artifacts  
• Supporting conservative risk and consequence framing  
• Providing confidence and uncertainty indicators  
• Highlighting state-dependent public health and operational constraints  

AI does not:

• Trigger lifecycle or operational state transitions  
• Execute treatment, chemical, or safety actions  
• Interpret water quality or compliance thresholds  
• Override human authority  
• Operate outside CAI Engine governance  

All AI outputs are explicitly bounded, explainable, auditable, and treated as advisory inputs
only.

AI behavior is subject to:

• CAI-Core-Integrity-and-Self-Defense-Governance  
• CAI-Core-Operational-State-Model  
• CAI USA AI governance constraints  
• Mandatory human-in-the-loop validation  

## Inputs to Decision Support

Decision support consumes:

• CCES-normalized telemetry  
• State-aware correlation artifacts  
• System and service operational state indicators (non-control)  
• Maintenance, inspection, and configuration context  
• Public health sensitivity annotations  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  

No single signal is sufficient to drive decisions.

## Decision Support Outputs

CAI produces structured decision-support artifacts including:

• Summary of observed conditions  
• System and service operational state context  
• Conservative risk and consequence framing  
• Possible response options for human consideration  
• Explicit non-recommended actions  
• Confidence and uncertainty indicators  

Outputs are descriptive, not prescriptive.

## Human Authority Enforcement

All decision-support outputs require explicit human review and acknowledgment by authorized
personnel.

CAI does not:

• Trigger enforcement  
• Advance lifecycle states  
• Approve actions  
• Override public water system operational authority  

Human decision-makers retain full accountability.

## Evidence and Auditability

Decision-support artifacts generate immutable evidence including:

• Inputs considered  
• Correlation rationale  
• AI-assisted analytical references  
• Time, state, and governance context  
• Human acknowledgment requirement  

Evidence supports audit, investigation, and forensic reconstruction.

## Water and Wastewater–Specific Constraints

In Water and Wastewater environments:

• Public health protection overrides remediation urgency  
• Operational continuity dominates decision framing  
• Ambiguity defaults to observation and documentation  
• Human review is mandatory prior to escalation  

Decision support must never introduce operational or public health pressure.
