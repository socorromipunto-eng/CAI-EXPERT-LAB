# CAI USA — Transportation Systems Labs (Non-Normative)

## Overview

This directory contains **non-normative laboratory artifacts** for the **CAI USA Transportation
Systems sector**. These laboratories demonstrate how CAI concepts are applied within
transportation-support environments in a **public-safety–first, operationally conservative,
and strictly human-governed** manner.

The Transportation Systems labs exist to validate **design intent**, **governance boundaries**,
**evidentiary handling**, and **explicit AI non-autonomy** without modifying CAI CORE, the
CAI USA baseline, or asserting regulatory, safety, or operational compliance.

---

## Non-Normative Scope

All artifacts contained in this directory are **laboratory demonstrations only**.

They:
- Do **not** define traffic control, dispatch, routing, or signaling behavior
- Do **not** authorize interaction with live transportation control systems
- Do **not** assert compliance with DOT, TSA, FAA, FRA, or other authorities
- Do **not** authorize operational or configuration changes
- Do **not** replace transportation safety governance or oversight

Normative CAI documentation remains authoritative at all times.

---

## Purpose of Transportation Systems Labs

The Transportation Systems laboratories are designed to demonstrate:

- CCES-compliant telemetry normalization for transportation-support environments
- State-aware correlation under public safety and continuity constraints
- Governed telemetry flow and evidence generation
- **Strictly advisory, human-governed decision support**
- Explicit **AI governance and non-autonomy**

These labs emphasize that **public safety, operational continuity, and human authority
dominate all analysis and decision-support activities**.

---

## Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence is present in all Transportation Systems labs as a **governed
analytical support component**.

Across all Transportation Systems laboratories:
- AI is **strictly advisory**
- AI does **not** control traffic, signaling, or dispatch
- AI does **not** advance lifecycle states
- AI does **not** trigger operational or safety actions
- AI does **not** override human authority
- AI behavior is **bounded, explainable, and auditable**

AI exists to support human understanding in safety-critical transportation contexts, not to
automate decisions.

## Transportation Systems Laboratory Artifacts

The Transportation Systems sector includes the following laboratory artifacts:

- **CCES Normalization**  
  `CAI-USA-Transportation-Systems-Lab-CCES-Normalization.md`  
  Demonstrates deterministic normalization of transportation-sector telemetry into CCES
  while preserving public safety context, operational dependencies, and evidentiary
  traceability.

- **State-Aware Correlation**  
  `CAI-USA-Transportation-Systems-Lab-State-Aware-Correlation.md`  
  Demonstrates correlation of CCES-normalized telemetry with explicit awareness of system
  state, service dependencies, and public safety constraints.

- **Telemetry Flow and Evidence**  
  `CAI-USA-Transportation-Systems-Lab-Telemetry-Flow-and-Evidence.md`  
  Demonstrates governed end-to-end telemetry flow and evidence generation, ensuring chain-of-
  custody, integrity, and audit readiness in transportation environments.

- **Human-Governed Decision Support**  
  `CAI-USA-Transportation-Systems-Lab-Human-Governed-Decision-Support.md`  
  Demonstrates strictly advisory, explainable decision support with mandatory human authority
  and explicit non-autonomy.
