## Purpose

The purpose of this README is to establish the Food and Agriculture sector directory as the authoritative entry point for the CAI USA Food and Agriculture sector overlay.  
This sector overlay applies the Cybersecurity Artificial Intelligence Framework to environments where cyber disruption can directly affect food safety, food availability, public health, economic stability, and national resilience.  
The overlay preserves CAI CORE semantics and the CAI USA baseline while introducing sector-specific assumptions, constraints, and evidentiary expectations appropriate for food and agriculture systems.

## Sector Positioning and Criticality

The Food and Agriculture sector is treated as a first-class CAI USA sector due to its role in sustaining population health, economic continuity, and supply-chain stability.  
This sector spans farming operations, processing facilities, storage and distribution systems, food manufacturing, agricultural technology platforms, and interconnected logistics and support services.  
The sector is characterized by heterogeneous environments combining IT, OT, ICS, CPS, legacy systems, seasonal operations, geographically distributed assets, and complex supply-chain trust relationships.  
Cyber incidents in this sector may produce cascading effects across public health, emergency services, transportation, energy dependency, and economic confidence.

## Interoperability Expectations

This sector overlay must remain interoperable with the CAI USA baseline and the CAI Engine execution model.  
Telemetry and event data must conform to CAI-USA-CCES and the CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, including sector profiling, normalization rules, and evidence integrity requirements.  
AI governance must refine CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance while explicitly prohibiting autonomous enforcement actions and preserving CAI CORE integrity and contamination-prevention rules.  
Operational execution must remain compatible with CAI-USA-Operations-Playbooks and CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration without redefining lifecycle semantics.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

This sector overlay inherits all authoritative CAI CORE semantics, including lifecycle stages, taxonomy, integrity governance, evolution governance, and non-claim posture.  
No CAI CORE concepts are redefined or overridden by this directory.  
CAI CORE remains authoritative for framework meaning, governance boundaries, and contamination-prevention controls.

## Relationship to CAI ENGINE

This sector overlay is executed through the CAI Engine under mandatory governance enforcement and human authority thresholds.  
The CAI Engine enforces policy, safety constraints, evidence capture, and state transitions defined by this overlay but does not invent sector rules or bypass operational authority.  
All high-impact actions remain bounded by deterministic behavior, reversibility expectations, and auditable approval paths.

## Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

This sector overlay extends the CAI USA baseline without redefining it.  
It operates in alignment with CAI-USA-Definition, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, CAI-USA-Secure-by-Design-Controls, CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration, and CAI-USA-Traceability-Matrix.

## Explicit Non-Claims

This sector overlay does not assert compliance with USDA, FDA, DHS, CISA, NIST, or any other authority.  
It does not certify systems, approve operational changes, authorize food safety actions, or replace regulatory, safety, or inspection processes.  
It provides a structured cybersecurity operational overlay only.
