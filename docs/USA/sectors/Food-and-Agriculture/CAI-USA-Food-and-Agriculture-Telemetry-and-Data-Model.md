This document defines the Food and Agriculture sector–specific telemetry and data model under the CAI USA baseline. It does not assert compliance, certification, accreditation, or regulatory approval.

Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the telemetry, data collection, normalization, and evidence requirements required to support CAI operations within the Food and Agriculture sector.

This telemetry model ensures visibility, integrity, traceability, and audit readiness across food production, processing, storage, and distribution environments while preserving operational continuity and safety.

Scope

This document applies to telemetry generated by IT, OT, ICS, cyber-physical systems, sensors, control platforms, logistics systems, and supporting digital services used within Food and Agriculture operations.

It refines the CAI USA telemetry baseline and aligns with the Common CAI Event Schema (CCES) without redefining CAI CORE semantics.

Telemetry Design Principles

Telemetry in Food and Agriculture environments is governed by the following principles:

Telemetry collection must not disrupt production, processing, or distribution operations.

Integrity and provenance of telemetry data are mandatory.

Telemetry must support forensic reconstruction, audit defensibility, and lifecycle decision support.

Data normalization must preserve sector context and operational meaning.

Gaps in telemetry coverage must be explicitly identified and managed.

Telemetry Domains

Food and Agriculture telemetry includes, but is not limited to, the following domains:

Production and processing telemetry capturing system state, configuration changes, and operational events.

ICS/OT telemetry including controller status, sensor readings, command execution, and safety-related events.

Cold-chain and storage telemetry capturing temperature, humidity, equipment status, and alarms.

Logistics and distribution telemetry capturing availability, routing, system health, and service continuity indicators.

Identity and access telemetry capturing authentication events, privilege usage, and access anomalies.

Security telemetry capturing alerts, detections, integrity violations, and policy enforcement actions.

CCES Alignment

All telemetry must be normalized using the Common CAI Event Schema (CCES).

Mandatory CCES fields include asset identifiers, sector profile identifiers, event type, timestamp, confidence indicators, integrity status, and provenance metadata.

Sector-specific extensions may include production stage, process context, storage condition indicators, and supply-chain role identifiers.

CCES normalization must not remove or obscure original evidence.

Integrity and Evidence Requirements

Telemetry must be protected against tampering, loss, and unauthorized modification.

Evidence artifacts must be retained in accordance with governance and investigation requirements.

Chain-of-custody for critical events must be preservable and reconstructable.

Loss of telemetry integrity is treated as a security-relevant event.

AI Consumption Constraints

AI systems may consume telemetry for correlation, anomaly detection, and decision support.

Telemetry used for AI inference must be validated for integrity and relevance.

AI outputs must preserve traceability to source telemetry and CCES records.

AI may not suppress, modify, or discard raw telemetry.

Data Retention and Access Governance

Telemetry retention periods must be defined and documented.

Access to telemetry data must follow least-privilege principles.

Sensitive operational telemetry must be access-controlled and monitored.

Cross-sector or cross-context telemetry reuse must be explicitly authorized.

Relationship to CAI CORE

This telemetry model inherits all telemetry and evidence semantics defined in CAI CORE.

CAI CORE remains authoritative for data governance, integrity protections, and lifecycle integration.

Relationship to CAI ENGINE

The CAI Engine enforces telemetry ingestion, validation, normalization, and evidence handling.

The Engine ensures telemetry supports deterministic execution, auditability, and policy evaluation.

Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

This document extends CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model for Food and Agriculture use.

It remains consistent with CAI-USA-Definition, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Secure-by-Design-Controls, CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, and CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration.

Explicit Non-Claims

This document does not assert compliance with USDA, FDA, DHS, CISA, NIST, or any other authority.

It does not prescribe specific tooling or mandate data retention durations.

It defines a sector-specific telemetry and data model only.
