## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define authoritative, auditable, and governance-enforced operational playbooks for applying the CAI lifecycle within Government environments.  
These playbooks translate CAI CORE intent into controlled operational actions suitable for public-sector systems that require accountability, legal defensibility, continuity of government functions, and strict human authority.

This document ensures that cybersecurity operations in Government contexts remain deterministic, evidence-driven, and subordinate to institutional governance.

## Scope

These playbooks apply exclusively to Government environments as defined in CAI-USA-Government-Sector-Definition.  
They operationalize CAI lifecycle stages without redefining CAI CORE semantics, CAI Engine authority, or CAI USA baseline governance.

## Operational Posture for Government Environments

Government operations prioritize continuity of public services, protection of sensitive information, and preservation of institutional trust.  
Adversaries may include criminal actors, hacktivists, insider threats, and nation-state actors.  
Operational response must preserve evidence, avoid unauthorized disruption, and ensure actions are legally and procedurally defensible.

Automation and AI may assist analysis and prioritization but must not autonomously execute high-impact actions.

## Playbook Governance Model

All playbooks are executed through the CAI Engine under non-bypassable governance controls.  
Each playbook defines preconditions, approval thresholds, execution constraints, and evidence requirements.  
Human authority is mandatory for actions affecting mission-critical systems, sensitive data, or inter-agency operations.

## MODEL Playbooks

MODEL playbooks establish authoritative visibility of Government assets, services, trust domains, data classifications, and inter-agency dependencies.  
Activities include validation of asset inventories, mapping of service ownership, identification of sensitive data flows, and documentation of trust assumptions.  
Outputs establish the baseline for risk prioritization and governance enforcement.

## HARDEN Playbooks

HARDEN playbooks define procedures for verifying and enforcing secure-by-design controls.  
Actions include configuration validation, access control review, segmentation enforcement, cryptographic hygiene checks, and integrity baseline confirmation.  
All remediation actions are staged, approved, and reversible.

## DETECT Playbooks

DETECT playbooks define procedures for identifying anomalies using CCES-normalized telemetry.  
Detection emphasizes behavioral deviations, integrity violations, unauthorized access attempts, and policy enforcement failures.  
Ambiguous signals trigger observation and enrichment rather than immediate disruption.

## ISOLATE Playbooks

ISOLATE playbooks define controlled containment actions to limit impact while preserving service continuity.  
Isolation actions are selective and minimize disruption to public services.  
Broad isolation requires explicit approval and impact assessment.

## DECEIVE Playbooks

DECEIVE playbooks define the controlled use of deception techniques for detection and intelligence purposes.  
Deception is restricted to approved environments and must not expose sensitive data or disrupt legitimate operations.  
Outputs support detection refinement and threat analysis only.

## EVICT Playbooks

EVICT playbooks define procedures for removing adversary presence and revoking compromised access.  
Eviction actions require evidence sufficiency, governance approval, and readiness validation.  
Eviction is executed conservatively to avoid service disruption or loss of investigative value.

## RESTORE Playbooks

RESTORE playbooks define procedures for returning systems to trusted operational baselines.  
Restoration emphasizes integrity verification, configuration validation, and monitored return to service.  
Post-restoration monitoring is mandatory.

## Human Authority and Escalation

All high-impact actions require explicit human authorization.  
Escalation paths may include cybersecurity leadership, legal counsel, agency leadership, and inter-agency coordination authorities.  
Human judgment is final for actions with legal, public, or national impact.

## Evidence and Accountability

Execution of playbooks generates auditable evidence including telemetry references, decision rationale, approval authority, execution context, and outcomes.  
Evidence supports audits, investigations, oversight reviews, and legal proceedings.  
Evidence integrity and traceability are mandatory.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

These playbooks inherit CAI CORE lifecycle semantics, execution models, integrity governance, contamination prevention rules, and audit principles.  
No CAI CORE concepts are redefined.

## Relationship to CAI ENGINE

The CAI Engine orchestrates playbook execution, enforces governance and safety gates, and captures evidence.  
The Engine does not override Government authority or institutional decision-making.

## Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

These playbooks refine CAI-USA-Operations-Playbooks for Government use.  
They operate alongside CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, and CAI-USA-Traceability-Matrix.
