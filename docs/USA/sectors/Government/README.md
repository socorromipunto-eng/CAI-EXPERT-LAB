## Purpose

The purpose of this directory is to define and contain the Government sector overlay under the CAI USA framework.  
The Government sector is treated as a first-class, high-criticality sector due to its responsibility for public administration, national governance, citizen services, inter-agency coordination, policy execution, and stewardship of sensitive information and critical digital services.

Cyber incidents affecting government environments may undermine public trust, disrupt essential services, expose sensitive information, degrade national security posture, or cascade across other critical infrastructure sectors.

This directory establishes the authoritative entry point for applying the Cybersecurity Artificial Intelligence Framework coherently within Government environments while preserving CAI CORE semantics, CAI USA baseline governance, interoperability principles, and strict non-claim posture.

## Sector Scope

The Government sector under CAI USA encompasses systems, environments, and operational domains operated or overseen by federal, state, local, tribal, and territorial government entities.

This includes administrative IT systems, citizen-facing digital services, identity and access platforms, inter-agency data exchanges, policy and regulatory systems, secure communications, cloud and hybrid environments, supporting data centers, and mission-support systems whose compromise could impact governance, public services, or national resilience.

This sector definition intentionally avoids defining statutory authority, regulatory compliance, certification, or authorization processes, which remain external to CAI.

## Sector Characteristics

Government environments are characterized by heterogeneous architectures, legacy systems, multi-agency dependencies, complex identity and trust relationships, and long-lived data assets.

Operations must balance confidentiality, integrity, availability, transparency, accountability, and legal oversight.  
Decision-making authority is distributed across organizational, legal, and political structures.  
Operational changes are subject to governance, documentation, and approval processes.

Cybersecurity operations must preserve evidence, auditability, and public accountability while maintaining continuity of essential services.

## Threat and Risk Context

Threats relevant to the Government sector include compromise of citizen data, identity abuse, disruption of digital public services, manipulation of policy or regulatory systems, espionage, influence operations, supply-chain compromise, and exploitation of inter-agency trust relationships.

Threat modeling prioritizes consequence, systemic impact, governance disruption, and public trust erosion over exploit novelty or frequency.  
Assumed breach is handled conservatively, emphasizing containment, monitoring, evidence preservation, and lawful response.

## AI and Automation Sensitivity

Government environments increasingly rely on automation and AI-assisted analytics for detection, prioritization, service optimization, and decision support.

Under CAI USA, AI usage in Government contexts must remain explicitly governed, explainable, auditable, and subordinate to human and institutional authority.  
Autonomous AI decision-making that may affect citizen rights, legal outcomes, policy enforcement, or essential services is not permitted.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

All Government sector artifacts inherit CAI CORE lifecycle semantics, execution models, integrity governance, contamination prevention rules, and evolution controls.  
The CAI seven-stage lifecycle is applied without modification.  
CAI CORE remains authoritative for taxonomy, operational state modeling, integrity protection, and framework governance.

No CAI CORE concepts are redefined or overridden within this sector.

## Relationship to CAI ENGINE

The Government sector is executed through the CAI Engine under enforced governance, policy evaluation, evidence generation, and mandatory human oversight.  
The CAI Engine orchestrates execution but does not override legal authority, administrative governance, or institutional decision-making structures.

## Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

This sector overlay extends the CAI USA baseline without redefining it.  
It operates alongside CAI-USA-Definition, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, CAI-USA-Secure-by-Design-Controls, CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration, and CAI-USA-Traceability-Matrix.

Sector-specific documents refine assumptions and constraints while preserving USA baseline semantics and interoperability.
