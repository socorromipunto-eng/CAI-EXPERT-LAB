This directory defines the Water and Wastewater sector under the CAI USA baseline and does not assert compliance, certification, accreditation, or regulatory approval.

Purpose

The purpose of the Water and Wastewater sector directory is to define a dedicated, first-class sector overlay within the CAI USA framework for environments responsible for drinking water treatment, wastewater treatment, distribution systems, monitoring infrastructure, and supporting operational technologies.

Water and wastewater systems are safety-critical and population-impacting. Cyber incidents affecting this sector may result in loss of potable water, environmental damage, public health crises, and cascading disruption of other critical infrastructure sectors.

This directory establishes the authoritative entry point for applying CAI coherently within Water and Wastewater environments while preserving CAI CORE semantics, USA baseline governance, and strict non-claim posture.

Sector Scope

The Water and Wastewater sector under CAI USA includes, but is not limited to:

Drinking water treatment facilities
Wastewater treatment plants
Water distribution and collection networks
Pumping stations and reservoirs
Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems
ICS/OT environments controlling physical processes
Supporting IT systems for monitoring, reporting, and coordination

This sector definition intentionally avoids defining regulatory compliance, certification, or operational authority, which remain external to CAI.

Sector Characteristics

Water and wastewater systems exhibit:

Strong dependence on ICS/OT and physical processes
Continuous operation requirements with limited downtime tolerance
Geographically distributed assets
Long-lived infrastructure and heterogeneous technologies
Direct impact on public health and environmental safety

Cybersecurity failures in this sector can result in delayed detection of contamination, loss of service, or unsafe operating conditions.

Risk and Threat Context

Threats relevant to the Water and Wastewater sector include:

Manipulation of treatment processes
Disruption of water distribution or wastewater collection
Unauthorized control of pumps, valves, and sensors
Supply-chain compromise of control system components
Insider misuse or credential compromise
Cascading impact to healthcare, emergency services, and energy sectors

Threat modeling emphasizes consequence-driven analysis, safety impact, and systemic risk over exploit novelty.

AI and Automation Sensitivity

AI-assisted analytics may be used to support anomaly detection, process monitoring, and risk prioritization.

Under CAI USA, AI usage in Water and Wastewater environments must remain explainable, auditable, and subordinate to human authority. Autonomous control of treatment processes or physical assets is explicitly prohibited.

Relationship to CAI CORE

This sector overlay inherits all lifecycle stages, governance principles, integrity protections, contamination prevention rules, and evolution controls defined in CAI CORE.

CAI CORE remains authoritative for lifecycle semantics, taxonomy, execution models, and framework integrity governance.

Relationship to CAI ENGINE

The Water and Wastewater sector is executed through the CAI Engine under strict safety gating and governance controls.

The CAI Engine enforces execution boundaries but does not replace operational authority over physical processes or environmental safety decisions.

Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

This directory extends the CAI USA baseline without redefining it.

It operates alongside CAI-USA-Definition, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, CAI-USA-Secure-by-Design-Controls, CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, CAI-USA-ICS-OT-Safety-Gating, CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration, and CAI-USA-Traceability-Matrix.
