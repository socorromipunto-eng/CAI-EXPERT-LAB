## Overview

The Energy sector is treated as a first-class sector under CAI USA due to its critical role in public safety, economic stability, and national security.  
Energy environments rely heavily on ICS/OT systems and exhibit safety-dominant operational constraints, long asset lifecycles, and tightly coupled dependencies between cyber and physical processes.

This sector overlay defines how CAI is applied within Energy environments without redefining CAI CORE or the CAI USA baseline.

## Sector Rationale

The Energy sector warrants independent treatment due to its unique risk profile, including systemic impact, cascading failure potential, and high-consequence outcomes from cyber incidents.  
Cybersecurity actions in this sector must prioritize safety, reliability, and controlled operation over rapid response or aggressive automation.

This sector is distinct from general industrial or IT environments and must be evaluated independently.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

All Energy sector artifacts inherit CAI CORE semantics, lifecycle stages, execution model, and governance principles.  
No Energy sector document redefines CAI CORE concepts or operational intent.

## Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

The Energy sector overlay extends the CAI USA baseline and inherits its transversal profiles, including ICS/OT safety assumptions, telemetry models, AI governance, and threat modeling integration.

Sector documents refine assumptions and constraints without altering USA baseline semantics.

## Sector Authorities and Context

While CAI does not assert regulatory compliance, the Energy sector operates within an ecosystem influenced by federal and national authorities such as DOE, DHS, CISA, FERC, and NERC.  
These references provide contextual relevance only and do not constitute claims of alignment or certification.
