## Purpose

The purpose of this directory is to establish the Nuclear sector as a first-class, independently governed sector within the CAI USA framework. Due to its extreme safety-critical nature, long-lived assets, national security implications, and catastrophic consequence potential, the Nuclear sector requires explicit treatment distinct from general Energy or Industrial sectors.

## Scope

The Nuclear sector under CAI USA encompasses environments and systems associated with nuclear reactors, nuclear materials handling, fuel cycle operations, radioactive waste management, and supporting operational and mission-support systems. This includes associated IT, OT, ICS, and cyber-physical environments whose compromise could directly or indirectly impact nuclear safety, environmental protection, or national security.

## Sector Characteristics

Nuclear environments operate under the highest safety and assurance constraints. System behavior must be deterministic, conservative, and fully accountable. Operational changes are tightly controlled, extensively reviewed, and subject to human authority. Automation is permitted only when bounded by explicit safety gates and governance mechanisms.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

This sector inherits all lifecycle stages, architectural principles, integrity governance, and evolution rules defined in CAI CORE. No CAI CORE concepts are redefined within this sector.

## Relationship to CAI ENGINE

Execution within the Nuclear sector is performed through the CAI Engine under reinforced safety gating and mandatory human oversight. The CAI Engine enforces policy and execution constraints but does not override nuclear safety authority or regulatory processes.

## Relationship to CAI USA Baseline

This sector overlay extends the CAI USA baseline without redefining it. It operates in conjunction with CAI-USA-ICS-OT-Safety-Gating, CAI-USA-AI-Governance-and-Assurance, CAI-USA-CCES, CAI-USA-Telemetry-and-Data-Model, and CAI-USA-Threat-Model-Integration.

## Explicit Non-Claims

This directory and its contents do not assert compliance with NRC, DOE, DHS, DoD, CISA, NIST, or any other authority. It does not certify systems, authorize operational changes, or replace regulatory, safety, or licensing processes.
