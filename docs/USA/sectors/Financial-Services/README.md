## Purpose

The purpose of the Financial Services sector directory is to define a dedicated, first-class sector overlay within the CAI USA framework for environments that manage, process, transmit, or depend upon financial transactions, monetary value, trust relationships, and systemic economic integrity.

Financial Services is treated as a critical and highly complex sector due to its systemic interdependencies, real-time operational requirements, reliance on cryptographic trust, high exposure to adversarial manipulation, and increasing use of automated and AI-assisted decision systems.

This directory establishes the authoritative entry point for applying CAI coherently to Financial Services environments while preserving CAI CORE semantics, USA baseline governance, and strict non-claim posture.

## Sector Scope

The Financial Services sector under CAI USA includes, but is not limited to:

- Banking systems and payment infrastructures
- Clearing, settlement, and interbank transfer systems
- Capital markets, trading platforms, and exchanges
- Digital payment services and fintech platforms
- Identity, authentication, and trust services supporting financial operations
- Supporting IT, cloud, data, and analytics environments whose compromise could impact financial integrity, availability, or confidence

This sector definition intentionally avoids defining regulatory compliance, certification, licensing, or supervisory authority, which remain external to CAI.

## Sector Characteristics

Financial Services environments are characterized by:

- High availability and low-latency operational requirements
- Strict integrity and non-repudiation expectations
- Heavy reliance on cryptographic controls and identity trust
- Complex, highly interconnected transaction flows
- Real-time fraud detection and risk scoring mechanisms
- Widespread use of automated and AI-assisted decision support

Failures or compromises in this sector can propagate rapidly across organizations, markets, and other critical infrastructure sectors.

## Risk and Threat Context

Threats relevant to the Financial Services sector include:

- Fraud and transaction manipulation
- Identity compromise and credential abuse
- Abuse or manipulation of automated decision systems
- Integrity attacks against transaction records and ledgers
- Supply-chain compromise of software and service providers
- Coordinated attacks targeting availability and trust confidence

Threat modeling in this sector prioritizes systemic impact, financial integrity, and trust erosion over isolated technical incidents.

## AI and Automation Sensitivity

Financial Services represents one of the highest-risk sectors for misuse or overreach of Artificial Intelligence.

AI systems in this sector are frequently used for:

- Fraud detection and anomaly identification
- Risk scoring and prioritization
- Transaction monitoring and behavioral analysis

Under CAI USA, AI usage in Financial Services must remain explicitly governed, explainable, and subordinate to human authority. Autonomous financial decision-making, enforcement, or irreversible actions are not permitted.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

This sector overlay inherits all lifecycle stages, governance principles, integrity protections, and contamination prevention rules defined in CAI CORE.

CAI CORE remains authoritative for:

- Lifecycle semantics
- Operational state models
- Taxonomy
- Framework integrity and evolution governance

No CORE concepts are redefined or overridden within this sector.

## Relationship to CAI ENGINE

The Financial Services sector is executed through the CAI Engine under strict governance, evidence, and human oversight constraints.

The CAI Engine may orchestrate AI-assisted workflows and enforcement actions only within bounded, reversible, and auditable limits defined by sector-specific documents.

The Engine enforces policy but does not replace financial, legal, or organizational authority.
