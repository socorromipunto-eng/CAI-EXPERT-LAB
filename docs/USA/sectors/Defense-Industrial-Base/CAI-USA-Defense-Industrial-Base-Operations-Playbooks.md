## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define authoritative operational playbooks for applying the CAI lifecycle within Defense Industrial Base environments. These playbooks translate CAI intent into controlled, auditable, intelligence-aware, and legally defensible actions while preserving national security relevance, protection of controlled information, supply-chain trust, and human authority across all stages of the CAI lifecycle.

## Scope

This document applies exclusively to the Defense Industrial Base sector as defined in CAI-USA-Defense-Industrial-Base-Sector-Definition. It operationalizes CAI lifecycle stages for DIB environments without redefining CAI CORE semantics, CAI Engine execution authority, or CAI USA baseline governance.

Operational Posture for the Defense Industrial Base  
DIB operations assume advanced, persistent adversaries and long-term campaigns targeting intellectual property, manufacturing integrity, and trust relationships. Operational actions prioritize evidence preservation, intelligence value, controlled containment, and strategic approval. Automation and AI assist analysis and preparation but do not autonomously execute actions with strategic, contractual, or national security impact.

## Playbook Governance and Execution Model

Each playbook aligns to a CAI lifecycle stage and is executed through the CAI Engine under non-bypassable governance and safety gates. Playbooks are deterministic, reversible where possible, and evidence-generating. Approval thresholds, escalation paths, legal considerations, and decision ownership are defined prior to execution. All actions are auditable and traceable.

## MODEL Playbooks

MODEL playbooks establish authoritative visibility of DIB assets, trust domains, data classifications, supply-chain dependencies, development pipelines, manufacturing processes, and partner integrations. Activities include validation of asset inventories, mapping of build and deployment workflows, identification of controlled information flows, modeling of supplier and contractor access paths, and establishment of baseline trust assumptions. Outputs inform prioritization, safety gating, and strategic risk assessment.

## HARDEN Playbooks

HARDEN playbooks guide the application and verification of secure-by-design controls across enterprise IT, R&D, manufacturing, and OT environments. Activities include enforcement of least privilege, segmentation validation, configuration integrity checks, credential hygiene verification, and confirmation of supplier and vendor access constraints. Remediation actions are staged, approved, and reversible, with explicit consideration of operational impact and evidentiary preservation.

## DETECT Playbooks

DETECT playbooks define procedures for high-confidence anomaly detection using CCES-normalized telemetry. Detection emphasizes behavioral anomalies, integrity deviations, credential misuse, build pipeline manipulation, and subtle persistence indicative of advanced adversaries. Ambiguous indicators trigger observation, enrichment, and human analysis rather than immediate disruption.

## ISOLATE Playbooks

ISOLATE playbooks define controlled containment actions to limit adversary movement while preserving intelligence value and operational continuity. Isolation actions are selective and staged. Actions that could disrupt manufacturing, development, or partner operations require explicit approval and impact assessment. Broad or irreversible isolation is avoided unless strategically justified.

## DECEIVE Playbooks

DECEIVE playbooks define the controlled use of deception techniques within DIB environments to detect, mislead, and study adversaries. Deception is limited to non-production, non-sensitive environments and must not expose controlled information or disrupt legitimate operations. Deception outputs feed detection and intelligence analysis only.

## EVICT Playbooks

EVICT playbooks define procedures for removing adversary presence, revoking compromised access, and eliminating persistence. Eviction is treated as a high-impact action due to potential intelligence loss, contractual implications, and operational disruption. Eviction requires evidence sufficiency, strategic approval, and readiness validation prior to execution.

## RESTORE Playbooks

RESTORE playbooks guide recovery actions to return systems to validated and trusted baselines. Restoration emphasizes integrity verification, supply-chain trust revalidation, and assurance of clean state before resuming full operations. Post-restoration monitoring is mandatory to detect re-compromise or residual persistence.

## Human Authority and Strategic Escalation

All playbooks involving strategic, legal, or national security impact require explicit human authorization. Escalation paths include cybersecurity leadership, legal counsel, supply-chain governance, and executive authority as appropriate. Human judgment is final for high-consequence decisions.

## Evidence and Accountability

Execution of playbooks generates auditable evidence including telemetry references, confidence scores, decision rationale, approval authority, execution context, and outcomes. Evidence supports intelligence analysis, contractual review, and legal proceedings. Evidence integrity and provenance are mandatory.

## Interaction with CAI Lifecycle and Engine

Playbooks align with CAI lifecycle stages and are executed via the CAI Engine under governance enforcement and safety gates. The Engine orchestrates execution but does not override organizational authority, contractual obligations, or strategic decision processes. State transitions preserve determinism and auditability.

## Relationship to CAI CORE

These playbooks inherit CAI CORE lifecycle semantics, execution models, integrity governance, contamination prevention, and audit principles. They remain consistent with CAI-Core-Operational-State-Model and CAI-Core-Execution-Model.
