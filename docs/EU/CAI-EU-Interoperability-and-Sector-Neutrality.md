## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document establishes the interoperability and sector-neutrality
principles for the CAI EU regional baseline.

Its purpose is to ensure that AI-assisted cybersecurity governance within
European contexts:

- Remains coherent across sectors

- Avoids regulatory or operational silos

- Supports cross-sector collaboration and oversight

- Preserves consistent human governance and accountability

This document is non-normative and does not define sector-specific
requirements or implementation guidance.

## 2. Interoperability as a Governance Requirement

Within CAI EU, interoperability is treated as a governance requirement,
not a technical optimization.

Interoperability refers to the ability of different sectors, organizations,
and governance structures to:

- Share a common governance vocabulary

- Apply consistent accountability expectations

- Exchange evidence and reasoning without semantic distortion

- Coordinate oversight and review activities

Technical interoperability may support these goals, but does not replace
governance interoperability.

## 3. Sector Neutrality by Design

CAI EU is sector-neutral by design.

At this foundational level:

- No sector is prioritized or assumed

- No sector-specific workflows are embedded

- No regulatory instrument is treated as dominant

This neutrality ensures that CAI EU remains applicable across diverse
European environments, including but not limited to:

- Public administration

- Critical infrastructure and utilities

- Industry and manufacturing

- Finance and insurance

- Healthcare and life sciences

- Transportation and mobility

- Research, education, and innovation ecosystems

Sector-specific characteristics may be addressed only through explicit,
scoped sector overlays.

## 4. Sector Overlays and Inheritance Constraints

Sector overlays, if introduced, must:

- Explicitly inherit CAI Core semantics

- Explicitly inherit CAI EU foundational governance

- Preserve risk- and proportionality-based constraints

- Maintain human authority and non-autonomy of AI

- Avoid introducing incompatible governance assumptions

No sector overlay may:

- Redefine decision authority

- Lower accountability thresholds

- Introduce autonomous AI behavior

- Fragment interoperability across sectors

## 5. Cross-Sector Consistency and Systemic Awareness

European cybersecurity environments are increasingly interconnected and
interdependent.

CAI EU therefore expects that:

- Governance decisions consider cross-sector and systemic effects

- Evidence models support aggregation and comparison across sectors

- Risk reasoning remains consistent even when operational contexts differ

This supports coordinated oversight without enforcing uniform operational
responses.

## 6. Human Governance Across Sector Boundaries

Human authority and accountability remain uniform across all sectors.

Regardless of domain:

- AI systems support, but do not decide

- Humans remain accountable for outcomes

- Decision rationale must remain reviewable

Sector-specific complexity does not justify reduced human oversight.

## 7. Relationship to European Regulatory Diversity

CAI EU acknowledges that European sectors operate under diverse and
sometimes overlapping regulatory regimes.

However:

- CAI EU does not harmonize or reinterpret regulations

- CAI EU does not resolve regulatory conflicts

- CAI EU does not prioritize one regulatory regime over another

Instead, it provides a neutral governance substrate upon which regulated
entities may reason consistently within their legal obligations.

## 8. Non-Scope Clarifications

This document does not:

- Define sector classifications or taxonomies

- Provide interoperability tooling or interfaces

- Establish data exchange standards

- Mandate cross-sector operational coordination

- Introduce sector-specific compliance guidance

Such elements, if introduced, must appear in clearly scoped, non-normative
derivative documents.

## 9. Forward Constraint

All future CAI EU sector overlays and extensions must demonstrate:

- Preservation of sector-neutral governance principles

- Explicit inheritance from CAI EU foundational documents

- Maintenance of cross-sector interoperability

- Continued enforcement of human-governed AI

Deviation from these constraints undermines EU baseline coherence.
