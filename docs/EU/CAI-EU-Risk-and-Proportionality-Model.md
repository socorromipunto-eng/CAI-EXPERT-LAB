## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines a risk- and proportionality-based governance model
for the use of Artificial Intelligence in cybersecurity activities within
European contexts.

Its purpose is to establish how the degree of AI involvement is constrained
by risk, impact, and context, while preserving:

- Human authority and accountability

- Sector-neutral interoperability

- Consistency with existing European legal and regulatory environments

This document is non-normative and does not establish compliance criteria,
certification thresholds, or regulatory interpretations.

## 2. European Context and Regulatory Environment

The European Union has established a comprehensive legal and regulatory
framework governing data protection, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence,
including binding instruments applicable across multiple sectors.

CAI EU acknowledges the existence and authority of these instruments.

However:

- CAI EU does not reinterpret regulatory text

- CAI EU does not map or claim compliance

- CAI EU does not replace legal analysis or obligations

This framework operates as a technical and governance reference designed
to support organizations operating within regulated environments, not to
define regulatory outcomes.

## 3. Risk-Governed AI Involvement

Within CAI EU, risk governs AI involvement, not capability.

This means:

- The more significant the potential impact, the more constrained AI usage becomes

- Increased risk requires increased human oversight

- Automation does not scale linearly with technical capability

AI involvement is always subordinate to human judgment, regardless of
sector, technology, or organizational maturity.

## 4. Proportionality as a Core Governance Principle

Proportionality is treated as a governing principle, not a technical parameter.

In CAI EU:

- Low-impact contexts may permit broader analytical AI assistance

- High-impact contexts require tighter human control and stronger evidentiary support

- AI autonomy is never introduced, regardless of proportionality

Proportionality ensures that AI assistance remains context-aware, bounded,
and accountable.

## 5. Human Authority and Decision Ownership

All cybersecurity decisions supported by AI within CAI EU:

- Are owned by identifiable human roles

- Require explicit human validation

- Must be explainable at a level appropriate to the decision impact

AI systems may inform decisions but cannot originate, authorize, or execute
final actions.

This principle applies uniformly across all sectors.

## 6. Evidence, Traceability, and Reviewability

Risk-sensitive contexts require stronger evidence and traceability.

CAI EU expects that:

- Decision rationale is preserved proportionally to impact

- AI-generated insights can be reviewed post hoc

- Human decisions remain reconstructable and auditable

Evidence requirements scale with risk and potential harm, not with system
complexity.

## 7. Sector-Neutral Interoperability

This model is explicitly sector-neutral.

It applies across, including but not limited to:

- Public administration

- Critical infrastructure and utilities

- Industry and manufacturing

- Finance and insurance

- Healthcare and life sciences

- Transportation and logistics

- Research, education, and innovation ecosystems

Sector-specific requirements may exist externally, but CAI EU does not embed
sector-specific assumptions at this level.

Interoperability is preserved by enforcing common governance constraints
before sector overlays are introduced.

## 8. Systemic and Cross-Sector Risk Awareness

CAI EU recognizes that cybersecurity risks increasingly exhibit systemic
and cross-sector characteristics.

Accordingly:

- Risk assessment must consider cascading and cross-domain effects

- AI assistance must not amplify systemic fragility

- Proportional controls must remain consistent across sector boundaries

This approach supports coordinated risk reasoning without enforcing uniform
operational responses.

## 9. Non-Scope Clarifications

This document does not:

- Define risk taxonomies or thresholds

- Provide quantitative scoring models

- Establish automated escalation rules

- Introduce AI-driven enforcement mechanisms

- Create sector-specific governance rules

Such elements, if ever introduced, must appear in clearly scoped,
non-normative derivative documents.

## 10. Forward Constraint

All future CAI EU artifacts must demonstrate that:

- AI involvement is governed by risk and proportionality

- Human authority remains explicit and enforceable

- Sector interoperability is preserved

- Existing European legal frameworks are respected without reinterpretation

Deviation from these principles undermines regional coherence.
