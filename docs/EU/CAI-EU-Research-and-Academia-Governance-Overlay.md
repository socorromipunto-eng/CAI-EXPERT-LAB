## 1. Purpose of This Overlay

This document defines a sector-specific governance overlay for the use of
AI-assisted cybersecurity within European research and academic contexts.

Its purpose is to articulate additional governance expectations applicable
to research institutions, academic environments, and publicly or privately
funded research activities, while preserving:

- CAI Core semantics

- CAI EU foundational governance principles

- Cross-sector interoperability

This overlay is non-normative and does not establish ethical approval
procedures, research methodologies, or regulatory compliance criteria.

## 2. Scope of Application

This overlay applies to research and academic environments, including but not
limited to:

- Universities and higher education institutions

- Public and private research organizations

- Research consortia and collaborative projects

- Experimental and exploratory cybersecurity research

- Academic contributions to standards and policy discussions

The overlay addresses governance considerations, not research execution
methods.

## 3. Research Context Risk Characteristics

Research and academic environments are characterized by:

- Exploratory and experimental activities

- Use of emerging or unvalidated techniques

- Interaction with sensitive data or critical systems

- Potential downstream impact on policy, standards, and practice

Accordingly, AI-assisted cybersecurity research requires explicit governance
constraints, even when activities are non-operational.

## 4. Human Authority and Research Accountability

Within research and academic contexts:

- AI systems remain tools for analysis and experimentation, not decision-makers

Human researchers retain full responsibility for research design, execution,
interpretation, and dissemination

## Accountability must be clearly attributable to identifiable roles

AI systems must not autonomously define research objectives, draw binding
conclusions, or validate results.

## 5. Proportionality in Experimental Contexts

Proportionality applies to research activities as follows:

- Higher-risk experiments require stronger governance and oversight

- Research involving sensitive systems or data requires increased justification

- Exploratory freedom does not override accountability

Experimental contexts do not justify reduced human control or diminished
traceability.

## 6. Evidence, Reproducibility, and Traceability

In research and academic contexts:

- Research assumptions and limitations must be documented

- AI-assisted analysis must remain explainable and reproducible

- Research outputs must preserve traceability to human decisions

Evidence integrity supports scientific rigor, peer review, and public trust.

## 7. Interoperability with Other Sectors

Research and academic activities must remain interoperable with:

- Public sector institutions

- Critical infrastructure operators

- Private-sector organizations

- Standards bodies and policy forums

This overlay avoids creating academic exceptionalism that would fragment
governance across sectors.

## 8. Relationship to European Regulatory and Ethical Frameworks

This overlay acknowledges that research activities may be subject to:

- Data protection obligations

- Ethical review processes

- Sector-specific legal constraints

However:

- No regulatory or ethical framework is reinterpreted

- No compliance claims are asserted

- No hierarchy of obligations is implied

The overlay supports responsible research governance, not regulatory
enforcement.

## 9. Non-Scope Clarifications

This overlay does not:

- Define research ethics approval processes

- Mandate data governance models

- Establish publication or dissemination rules

- Introduce automated research validation mechanisms

Such matters remain outside the scope of CAI EU.

## 10. Forward Constraint

Future research- and academia-specific extensions must demonstrate:

- Explicit inheritance from this overlay

- Preservation of human authority and accountability

- Respect for proportionality and traceability principles

- Continued cross-sector interoperability

Deviation from these constraints undermines EU baseline coherence.
