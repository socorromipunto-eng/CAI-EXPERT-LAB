1. Purpose and Role of This Document

This document establishes the foundational governance principles for the
European Union (EU) regional baseline of the Cybersecurity Artificial Intelligence
Framework (CAI).

Its purpose is to define how AI-assisted cybersecurity activities are governed
in European contexts, independently of sector, technology stack, or regulatory
instrument, while remaining fully consistent with CAI Core.

This document:

Is non-normative

Does not assert regulatory compliance

Does not introduce operational playbooks

Does not mandate architectures or tooling

Does not authorize autonomous AI behavior

Instead, it provides a governance anchor that all future CAI EU documents,
including sector overlays, must respect.

2. Authority Hierarchy

The authority hierarchy for CAI EU is strictly defined as follows:

CAI Core — Authoritative and immutable

CAI EU Foundational Governance — Regional governance interpretation

CAI EU Subsequent Documents — Derived and constrained artifacts

No CAI EU document may:

Redefine CAI Core semantics

Introduce autonomous AI authority

Override human decision accountability

Implicitly assert legal compliance

In the event of ambiguity or conflict, CAI Core always prevails.

3. Governance First Principle (EU Context)

CAI EU adopts a governance-first approach.

This means that:

Governance constraints are defined before sector use cases

Risk framing precedes operational execution

Accountability precedes efficiency

Human authority precedes automation

This reflects established European expectations regarding:

Proportionality

Subsidiarity

Fundamental rights protection

Systemic risk awareness

Operational efficiency is treated as secondary to governance integrity.

4. Human Authority and Non-Autonomy

Within CAI EU:

AI systems do not decide

AI systems do not authorize

AI systems do not enforce

AI systems do not act independently

AI may:

Support human decision-making

Provide analysis, correlation, and recommendation

Assist in evidence preparation

Enhance situational awareness

All final decisions remain under explicit human authority, with identifiable
roles and accountability.

No CAI EU artifact may dilute or obscure this principle.

5. Accountability and Traceability

CAI EU requires that AI-assisted cybersecurity activities support:

Clear attribution of human responsibility

Traceable decision rationale

Preservation of decision context

Evidence integrity suitable for audit and review

Traceability is treated as a governance requirement, not a technical feature.

The absence of perfect data or tooling does not justify loss of accountability.

6. Proportionality and Risk Sensitivity

CAI EU adopts proportionality as a core governance principle.

This implies that:

AI involvement must scale with assessed risk

Higher impact contexts require stronger human oversight

Evidence depth increases with decision criticality

Automation is constrained as risk increases

Proportionality is evaluated contextually, not mechanically.

CAI EU does not define risk thresholds; it defines how risk framing governs behavior.

7. Interoperability and Sector Neutrality

CAI EU is sector-neutral by design.

This document applies equally to:

Public administration

Critical infrastructure

Industry and manufacturing

Finance and insurance

Healthcare and life sciences

Research and academia

Emerging digital services

Sector-specific considerations may be introduced only through explicit sector overlays,
which must inherit and respect this foundational governance layer.

No sector overlay may:

Introduce autonomous AI authority

Reduce accountability requirements

Redefine proportionality principles

Fragment interoperability across sectors

8. Relationship to European Regulation

CAI EU:

Acknowledges the existence of European regulatory instruments

Does not reinterpret regulatory text

Does not claim alignment, compliance, or conformity

Does not substitute legal analysis or obligations

This framework operates adjacent to regulation, not inside it.

Its role is to support governed technical and organizational reasoning,
not regulatory enforcement.

9. Non-Scope Clarifications

This document does not:

Define compliance mappings

Provide conformity assessment guidance

Establish certification criteria

Authorize deployment patterns

Mandate technical controls

Such topics, if ever addressed, must appear in clearly scoped, non-normative
derivative documents.

10. Forward Constraint

All future CAI EU documents must demonstrate:

Explicit inheritance from this governance baseline

Preservation of CAI Core semantics

Respect for non-autonomy

Multisector applicability

Audit-defensible language

Deviation from these constraints invalidates regional coherence.
