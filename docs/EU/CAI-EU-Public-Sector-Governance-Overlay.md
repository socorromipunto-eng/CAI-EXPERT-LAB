## 1. Purpose of This Overlay

This document defines a sector-specific governance overlay for the use
of AI-assisted cybersecurity within European public sector contexts.

Its purpose is to articulate additional governance expectations
applicable to public administrations and public-interest bodies, while:

- Preserving CAI Core semantics

- Preserving CAI EU foundational governance

- Maintaining cross-sector interoperability

This overlay is non-normative and does not establish regulatory
compliance criteria, certification requirements, or operational mandates.

## 2. Scope of Application

This overlay applies to public-sector environments, including but not
limited to:

- Central and local government administrations

- Public agencies and authorities

- Publicly funded institutions

- Supranational and intergovernmental bodies operating within EU contexts

The overlay addresses governance considerations, not operational
deployment models.

## 3. Public Sector Governance Characteristics

Public-sector cybersecurity activities are characterized by:

- Heightened accountability to the public

- Legal and institutional transparency obligations

- Democratic oversight and scrutiny

- Long-term systemic and societal impact

Accordingly, AI-assisted cybersecurity in this sector requires stronger
governance constraints than purely commercial environments.

## 4. Human Authority and Democratic Accountability

Within the public sector:

- All AI-assisted cybersecurity decisions remain under explicit human authority

- Decision ownership must be attributable to identifiable public officials or roles

- Accountability mechanisms must support democratic and institutional oversight

AI systems must not replace or obscure human responsibility for public
sector actions.

## 5. Risk, Proportionality, and Public Impact

Risk assessment in public-sector contexts must consider:

- Potential impact on fundamental rights

- Societal and cross-sector consequences

- Public trust and legitimacy implications

As risk and public impact increase:

- Human oversight requirements increase

- Evidence and justification requirements increase

- AI assistance becomes more constrained

Automation is never expanded as a response to increased impact.

## 6. Transparency and Explainability Expectations

Public-sector use of AI-assisted cybersecurity requires:

- Decision rationales that are explainable at an institutional level

- Documentation suitable for audit, inquiry, and review

- Transparency mechanisms aligned with public accountability norms

Explainability is treated as a governance obligation, not merely a
technical feature.

## 7. Interoperability with Other Sectors

Public-sector governance must remain interoperable with:

- Critical infrastructure operators

- Regulated private-sector entities

- Research and academic institutions

This overlay does not introduce public-sector exceptionalism that would
fragment cross-sector governance coherence.

## 8. Relationship to European Regulatory Frameworks

This overlay acknowledges that public-sector entities operate under
established European legal and regulatory instruments.

However:

- No regulatory interpretation is provided

- No compliance claims are asserted

- No regulatory hierarchy is implied

This overlay supports governed reasoning within regulated environments,
not regulatory enforcement.

## 9. Non-Scope Clarifications

This overlay does not:

- Define procurement rules or policies

- Mandate technical architectures or tools

- Establish transparency reporting formats

- Introduce automated decision or enforcement mechanisms

Such elements fall outside the scope of CAI EU.

## 10. Forward Constraint

Future public-sector-specific extensions must demonstrate:

- Explicit inheritance from this overlay

- Preservation of human authority and accountability

- Consistency with CAI EU proportionality principles

- Continued cross-sector interoperability

Deviation from these constraints undermines EU baseline integrity.
