## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to formally declare that **CAI USA v0.1**, when combined with
the declared CAI Core v0.1 and the associated non-normative laboratory artifacts, constitutes
a **technically sufficient and internally consistent regional baseline** for the United
States context.

This declaration exists to eliminate ambiguity regarding whether additional artifacts,
extensions, or sector definitions are required to evaluate CAI USA as a complete reference
framework.

---

## Definition of CAI USA Baseline Sufficiency

CAI USA v0.1 is considered sufficient when the following conditions are met:

- CAI Core v0.1 is declared complete and authoritative
- The CAI USA baseline defines jurisdictional and operational constraints without modifying
  CAI Core semantics
- All priority sectors are represented through frozen sector overlays and traceability
  matrices
- CCES, telemetry, AI governance, and operational models are fully defined
- Sector-specific non-normative laboratories demonstrate execution fidelity and governance
- The baseline is frozen under a master change-control declaration

All of the above conditions are satisfied in CAI USA v0.1.

---

## Scope of CAI USA Baseline

The CAI USA baseline defines:

- Jurisdictional alignment for United States operational environments
- Sector overlays and traceability matrices aligned with federal priority sectors
- Telemetry, evidence, and CCES mappings appropriate for U.S. contexts
- AI governance and non-autonomy constraints aligned with U.S. expectations
- Operational playbooks and secure-by-design controls
- Non-normative laboratory demonstrations validating design intent

CAI USA is explicitly scoped as a **reference baseline**, not an enforcement or certification
framework.

---

## Explicit Non-Scope

CAI USA explicitly does **not**:

- Assert compliance with any U.S. regulation, standard, or mandate
- Replace regulatory frameworks such as NIST, CISA guidance, or CMMC
- Provide certification, accreditation, or authorization
- Define operational deployment instructions
- Require the use of extensions or optional modules
- Impose requirements on non-U.S. jurisdictions

---

## Relationship to CAI Core

CAI USA is derived from and subordinate to CAI Core.

- CAI Core remains authoritative for all semantics and governance
- CAI USA introduces no modifications to CAI Core definitions
- Any conflict defaults to CAI Core documentation

CAI USA exists as a regional adaptation layer only.

---

## Relationship to Laboratories

The CAI USA laboratories are non-normative artifacts designed to:

- Demonstrate execution fidelity of the CAI USA baseline
- Validate governance boundaries and non-autonomy principles
- Provide auditable, reviewable examples without asserting compliance

Laboratories are illustrative only and are not authoritative.

---

## Sufficiency Without Extensions

CAI USA v0.1 does not require any CORE or regional extensions to be considered sufficient.

The absence of extensions does not imply incompleteness or missing capability.

---

## Intended Use

CAI USA v0.1 is suitable for:

- Technical review by standards bodies and agencies
- Framework comparison and analysis
- Reference architecture discussions
- Research and academic evaluation
- Controlled pilot or design exploration

CAI USA v0.1 is **not** intended to be used as a compliance checklist or certification basis.
