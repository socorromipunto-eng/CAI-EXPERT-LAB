Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the mandatory Dual Audit Pipeline of the Cybersecurity Artificial Intelligence Framework (CAI).
The Dual Audit Pipeline establishes a non-bypassable verification mechanism that governs all changes, interpretations, and evolutions of CAI Core artifacts, including documentation, architecture, semantics, mappings, and AI-assisted outputs.

This pipeline ensures that no change to CAI is accepted, published, or operationalized without passing both technical validation and governance integrity review.
The Dual Audit Pipeline exists to protect CAI from accidental errors, unauthorized modifications, semantic drift, AI hallucinations, and adversarial or reputational manipulation.

Scope

The Dual Audit Pipeline applies to all CAI Core documents, all CAI Engine artifacts, all regional overlays including CAI-USA, all sector profiles, all extensions, and all derived or referenced materials that claim lineage from CAI.

No artifact is considered authoritative unless it has passed the Dual Audit Pipeline and is traceable through the Evolution Traceability Register.

Audit Model Overview

The Dual Audit Pipeline is composed of two mandatory and independent audit paths that must both succeed before any artifact is accepted.

The Technical Audit validates correctness, internal consistency, and operational soundness.
The Governance Audit validates authority, intent, integrity, scope, and non-misrepresentation.

Failure in either audit path results in rejection or quarantine of the artifact, regardless of technical merit or functional completeness.

Technical Audit

The Technical Audit evaluates whether a proposed artifact or change is technically correct and coherent with existing CAI Core definitions.

The Technical Audit verifies alignment with CAI lifecycle semantics, architectural boundaries, operational state models, taxonomy, and execution constraints.
It validates that no contradictions are introduced across documents and that references remain accurate and internally consistent.

For AI-assisted content, the Technical Audit verifies that generated material is grounded in existing Core definitions and does not introduce fabricated concepts, standards, or claims.

Governance Audit

The Governance Audit evaluates whether a proposed artifact or change preserves the integrity, authority, and ethical posture of the CAI framework.

The Governance Audit verifies that no implicit compliance, certification, or regulatory claims are introduced.
It ensures that scope boundaries are respected and that no sector, region, or tool is granted authority to reinterpret Core semantics.

The Governance Audit explicitly evaluates AI involvement to ensure that no AI-generated output alters framework meaning, authority, or governance rules without explicit human approval.

AI-Assisted Content Handling

Any artifact produced or modified with AI assistance is automatically subject to enhanced scrutiny under the Dual Audit Pipeline.

AI-generated content must be explicitly reviewed by a human CAI authority and validated against existing Core definitions.
AI systems are not authorized to approve, finalize, or publish CAI artifacts.

The Dual Audit Pipeline treats AI hallucination, overreach, or semantic distortion as integrity threats equivalent to adversarial manipulation.

Decision Authority

Final approval authority rests with designated CAI governance roles as defined in CAI Core documents.
No automated system, engine component, or external contributor may bypass or replace human audit authority.

Approval decisions must be documented, version-controlled, and traceable.

Relationship to Other Core Artifacts

This document operates under and in conjunction with CAI-Core-Integrity-and-Self-Defense-Governance.
It is enforced through Evolution-Traceability-Register and informed by Threat-Model-Framework-Integrity.

In case of conflict, Core Integrity governance prevails.

Enforcement

Artifacts that do not pass the Dual Audit Pipeline are not considered part of CAI, regardless of publication status.
Use of non-approved artifacts constitutes a violation of CAI governance rules.
